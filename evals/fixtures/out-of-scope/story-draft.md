# The Lighthouse Keeper's Daughter

Mira had not seen the mainland in three years.

The light kept its slow rotation above her, painting the cliff in long pulses of yellow. Below, the sea moved the way it always moved — gently, gently, then not gently at all — and she watched a gull settle on the rail and consider her with one cold eye before lifting away again.

She was not lonely. That was the thing the visitors never understood when the supply boat came in spring and they stood on the dock with their pity already arranged on their faces. She was not lonely. She had her father's books, and the light, and the slow weather, and the gull who came back each morning as if it had decided she was something it owned.

It would be enough. It had been enough.

Until the letter.
